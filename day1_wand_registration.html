The Academy of Magic & Marvels · Day 1

Wand Registration & the Three Trials

Your wand arrived today. It's obedient and completely literal: it does exactly what you say, which is not always what you meant. Register it, then pass the three trials: Speak, Repeat, Choose.

SPEAK · REPEAT · CHOOSE

YOU'LL NEED: VS Code open · a new file saved as day1.py · your Grimoire trace sheet

Today's target

I can store and trace values, weave a pattern with a loop, and write a gate that chooses. E5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

I can break a spell on purpose and read the traceback last line first. E5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Chunks explored
Chunk 1 · First incantation

Wake the wand

Type this one line into day1.py, exactly as written. Press Run (the triangle, top right) and watch the terminal.

print("The gates of the Academy open for you.")
Predict What exact characters appear? Will the quotation marks be part of it? Say your guess out loud, then run.
The words appear without the quotation marks. Quotes are instructions for the wand: they mark where your message starts and ends. Everything between them is called a string.
You should see 
The gates of the Academy open for you.
Chunk 2 · Misfire on purposeE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Break it, then read the smoke

Every mage misfires. Do it on purpose now, so the error message can never scare you again. Change print to pint and run:

pint("The gates of the Academy open for you.")
Predict Will anything print? What will the wand say instead, and where on the screen will you look first?
Nothing prints. The wand answers with a traceback. Read it last line first: the bottom line names the misfire, and here it says the wand knows no spell called pint. Fix the spelling and run again. You've just done what programmers do all day.
You should see (last line of the traceback) 
NameError: name 'pint' is not defined. Did you mean: 'print'?
Chunk 3 · The vesselE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Bottle a value, label the bottle

A variable is a labeled vessel: the name on the outside, one value inside. Watch the two prints closely. One has quotes and one doesn't.

wand_wood = "rowan"
print(wand_wood)
print("wand_wood")
Predict Two lines will appear. What exactly does each one say? Write both on your Grimoire sheet before you run.
First rowan, then wand_wood. Without quotes, the wand opens the vessel and pours out what's inside. With quotes, it just says those exact letters. This one difference explains half the misfires you'll ever see.
You should see 
rowan
wand_wood
Chunk 4 · One vessel, one valueE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Pour in, pour out

What happens when you put a second value into a vessel that already holds one?

sparks = 20
sparks = 15
print(sparks)
Predict Will the terminal show 20, 15, both, or 35? Trace it on your Grimoire sheet, one row per line.
15, and only 15. The = sign pours: whatever the vessel held before is gone, and the most recent pour wins. Nothing adds up unless you ask for math on purpose.
You should see 
15
Chunk 5 · The listening charmE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Make the wand ask a question

input() makes the program stop and listen. Whatever gets typed lands in the vessel on the left.

name = input("State your name, Apprentice: ")
print("Welcome,", name)
Predict Where exactly does the program pause and wait? After you type your name and press Enter, what ends up inside name?
It pauses at line 1, right after printing the question. The blinking cursor in the terminal is the wand listening. Whatever you type before pressing Enter becomes the value inside name, and line 2 pours it back out.
You should see (if the apprentice is Mira) 
State your name, Apprentice: Mira
Welcome, Mira
Chunk 6 · The text trap, and the counter-charmE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Why 12 × 3 makes 121212, and how int() fixes it

Ask for a number, multiply it, and watch closely. Type 12 when it asks. Then run the fixed version and type 12 again.

sparks = input("How many sparks? ")
print(sparks * 3)

# the counter-charm: convert at the gate
sparks = int(input("How many sparks? "))
print(f"Total power: {sparks * 3}")
Predict First run: 36, or something stranger? Second run: what changes, and what does the f before the quotes do?
First: 121212. input() always hands you text, and text times 3 repeats the letters. The wand did exactly what you said. Second: int() converts the text to a real number the moment it arrives, so the math works. The Academy calls this convert at the gate. And the f makes an f-string: anything inside { } gets swapped for its value.
You should see (typing 12 both times) 
How many sparks? 12
121212
How many sparks? 12
Total power: 36
Chunk 7 · Trial of RepeatingE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

for and range: one rule, many rows

A loop repeats your indented lines, and the vessel row is refilled each pass. Hand it to the repeat charm and a shape appears.

print("✦" * 5)

for row in range(1, 5):
    print("✦" * row)
Predict Line 1 first: five runes, or the number 5? Then the loop: sketch every row on your Grimoire sheet. Where does range(1, 5) start, and where does it stop?
Five runes, then a staircase of 1 to 4. Text times a number repeats the text, and range(1, 5) deals 1, 2, 3, 4: it starts at the first number and stops before the second. One small rule made every row. If your terminal shows hollow boxes, swap ✦ for *; the magic is identical.
You should see 
✦✦✦✦✦
✦
✦✦
✦✦✦
✦✦✦✦
Chunk 8 · The missing rowE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Misfire on purpose: the off-by-one

An apprentice wants a four-row staircase and writes this. Read it first. Something is short.

for row in range(1, 4):
    print("✦" * row)
Predict How many rows will actually weave, and which row is missing? Say why before you run.
Three rows. The four-rune row never weaves, because range(1, 4) stops before 4. This is the most famous misfire in programming, the off-by-one. The counter-curse: to reach the number you want, write one past it, range(1, 5).
You should see (one row short) 
✦
✦✦
✦✦✦
Chunk 9 · Trial of ChoosingE5-PRO-RD-?? ⚠

if and else: the two-door gate

An if line asks a yes-or-no question. The indented lines below it run only when the answer is yes, and else catches every no.

temp = 80
if temp > 75:
    print("The cauldron glows ember-bright.")
else:
    print("The brew sleeps. Add more flame.")
Predict Which one line prints at 80? Change temp to 60 and predict again before the second run. Could both lines ever print on the same run?
At 80, the glow. At 60, the sleep. And no: exactly one door opens per run. The wand takes one fork in the corridor and moves on.
You should see (with temp = 80) 
The cauldron glows ember-bright.
Chunk 10 · Three doors, flatE5-PRO-RD-?? ⚠

elif: the middle door

Three outcomes, one flat ladder. The wand checks each question in order and takes the first true door.

temp = 90
if temp < 50:
    print("Too cold: the brew crystallizes.")
elif temp > 85:
    print("Scorching: stand back!")
else:
    print("Steady heat. Keep stirring.")
Predict At 90: how many of the three lines print, and which one? What about at exactly 85?
Exactly one line, always. At 90 it's Scorching. At 85 both questions are false (85 is not less than 50, and 85 is not greater than 85), so the else door opens: Steady heat. Notice the ladder stays flat, three doors side by side, none hiding inside another. That's how gates are written at this Academy.
You should see (with temp = 90) 
Scorching: stand back!
Chunk 11 · The shelfE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

A list: one vessel, many flasks

A list is a shelf of flasks, and every flask has a number. The numbering starts at 0. Then reach for a flask that isn't there, on purpose.

shelf = ["moonpetal", "ashroot", "dewbark"]
print(shelf[0])
print(shelf[2])
print(len(shelf))
print(shelf[3])
Predict Three lines will print, and then something else happens. Which ingredient comes out of flask 0? What does len report? And flask 3: the last ingredient, or a misfire?
moonpetal, dewbark, then 3, and then the wand misfires. The flasks are numbered 0, 1, 2, so flask 3 doesn't exist. On a 3-flask shelf the last flask is number 2. The last number is always one less than the count. Counting from zero feels wrong for about a day. It wears off.
You should see (then the traceback's last line) 
moonpetal
dewbark
3
IndexError: list index out of range
Chunk 12 · The cauldron and the gateE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

random.choice and in: the shelf comes alive

Import the chance-magic once, at the top of the file. The cauldron picks a flask, and the word in asks the shelf a yes-or-no question. Run it twice: ask for ashroot, then gloomvine.

import random

shelf = ["moonpetal", "ashroot", "dewbark"]
print("The cauldron chooses:", random.choice(shelf))

ask = input("Which ingredient do you seek? ")
if ask in shelf:
    print("Fetch it from the open shelf.")
else:
    print("The Restricted Shelf stays sealed.")
Predict What lands in the cauldron's choice: an ingredient, or a flask number? Which door opens for ashroot, and which for gloomvine? What would typing Ashroot with a capital A do?
The cauldron hands you the ingredient itself, never its number, and it can repeat across runs; each choice is fresh and forgetful. ashroot opens the shelf, gloomvine hits the sealed door, and Ashroot stays sealed too: the wand matches letters exactly, and a capital A is a different letter.

The cauldron chooses: dewbark
Which ingredient do you seek? ashroot
Fetch it from the open shelf.

Which ingredient do you seek? gloomvine

Hinge question · stop here until you're sureE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

The two-part gate

Part 1: a program runs charge = 40 and then charge = 25. What does charge hold now?
Part 2: without running it, how many lines does for row in range(2, 5): print("✦" * row) weave, and how many runes are on the last line?

Part 1: 25, and only 25. A vessel holds one value, and = pours over whatever was there. Part 2: three lines, and the last holds four runes. range(2, 5) deals 2, 3, 4. If either part surprised you, re-trace Chunks 4 and 7 before the build. If you got both, the trials are passed.
Build brief · The Registration Scroll

Register, measure, and be assigned

Build a Registration Scroll program that:

1. Prints the Academy seal (2–3 lines, or a loop of one banner line).
2. Asks the apprentice's name with input().
3. Asks how many spell tokens they carry, converting with int().
4. Computes their sparks (each token holds 12) and prints one f-string scroll line.
5. Gates the result: over 30 sparks earns one message, otherwise another.
6. Lets the cauldron assign a Hall with random.choice.

Useful pieces: print() · input() · int() · f"..." · for / range() · if / else · random.choice()

Suggested start: seal first, then one question at a time. House law: one change per run.

import random

# --- Academy seal ---
for row in range(3):
    print("* * * * * * * * * * *")

# --- Registration desk ---
name = input("State your name, Apprentice: ")
tokens = int(input("How many spell tokens? "))
sparks = tokens * 12
print(f"{name}, your wand holds {sparks} sparks.")

# --- The measuring gate ---
if sparks > 30:
    print("An uncommonly bright wand. Handle it with care.")
else:
    print("A steady wand. It will grow with you.")

# --- The cauldron assigns ---
hall = random.choice(["Emberwing", "Frostveil", "Gloamlight"])
print(f"The cauldron assigns you to {hall} Hall.")
Yours doesn't have to match this. It has to run, and you have to be able to say what each line does.
