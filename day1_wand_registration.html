The Academy of Magic & Marvels · Day 1

Wand Registration

Your wand arrived today. It's obedient and completely literal: it does exactly what you say, which is not always what you meant.

ACADEMY OF MAGIC & MARVELS

YOU'LL NEED: VS Code open · a new file saved as day1.py · your Grimoire trace sheet (from your teacher)

Today's target

I can make the wand print exactly what I write. E5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

I can store a value in a variable and use what an apprentice types. E5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Chunks explored
Chunk 1 · First incantation

Wake the wand

Type this one line into day1.py, exactly as written. Then press the Run button (the triangle, top right) and watch the terminal at the bottom.

print("The gates of the Academy open for you.")
Predict Before you run it: what exact characters will appear in the terminal? Will the quotation marks be part of it? Say your guess out loud, then run.
The words appear without the quotation marks. Quotes are instructions for the wand: they mark where your message starts and ends. Everything between them is called a string.
You should see 
The gates of the Academy open for you.
Chunk 2 · Misfire on purposeE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Break it, then read the smoke

Every mage misfires. Today you do it on purpose, so the error message can never scare you again. Change print to pint and run:

pint("The gates of the Academy open for you.")
Predict Will anything print? What do you think the wand will say instead? Where on the screen will you look first?
Nothing prints. The wand answers with a traceback. Read it last line first: the bottom line names the misfire, and here it says the wand knows no spell called pint. Fix the spelling and run again. You've just done what programmers do all day.
You should see (last line of the traceback) 
NameError: name 'pint' is not defined. Did you mean: 'print'?
Chunk 3 · One line at a timeE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Chant the Academy seal

The wand reads your file the way you read a scroll: top to bottom, one line at a time. Add these three lines and run.

print("=== ACADEMY OF MAGIC & MARVELS ===")
print("      Apprentice Registration")
print("==================================")
Predict If you swapped line 1 and line 2, what would change in the terminal: the words, the order, or both? Decide, then try it.
Only the order changes: same words, different sequence. A program is a sequence, and the wand never skips ahead. Swap the lines back before you continue.
You should see 
=== ACADEMY OF MAGIC & MARVELS ===
      Apprentice Registration
==================================
Chunk 4 · The vesselE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Bottle a value, label the bottle

A variable is a labeled vessel: the name on the outside, one value inside. Watch the two prints closely. One has quotes and one doesn't.

wand_wood = "rowan"
print(wand_wood)
print("wand_wood")
Predict Two lines will appear. What exactly does each one say? Write both on your Grimoire sheet before you run.
First rowan, then wand_wood. Without quotes, the wand opens the vessel and pours out what's inside. With quotes, it just says those exact letters. This one difference explains half the misfires you'll ever see.
You should see 
rowan
wand_wood
Chunk 5 · One vessel, one valueE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Pour in, pour out

What happens when you put a second value into a vessel that already holds one?

sparks = 20
sparks = 15
print(sparks)
Predict Will the terminal show 20, 15, both, or 35? Trace it on your Grimoire sheet: one row per line, what does sparks hold after each?
15, and only 15. The = sign pours: whatever the vessel held before is gone, and the most recent pour wins. Nothing adds up unless you ask for math on purpose.
You should see 
15
Chunk 6 · The listening charmE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Make the wand ask a question

input() makes the program stop and listen. Whatever gets typed lands in the vessel on the left.

name = input("State your name, Apprentice: ")
print("Welcome,", name)
Predict Where exactly does the program pause and wait? And after you type your name and press Enter, what ends up inside name?
It pauses at line 1, right after printing the question. The blinking cursor in the terminal is the wand listening. Whatever you type before pressing Enter becomes the value inside name, and line 2 pours it back out.
You should see (if the apprentice is Mira) 
State your name, Apprentice: Mira
Welcome, Mira
Chunk 7 · The text trapE5-PRO-TR-?? ⚠

Why 12 × 3 makes 121212

Ask for a number, multiply it, and watch closely. Type 12 when it asks.

sparks = input("How many sparks for practice? ")
print(sparks * 3)
Predict You typed 12. Will the terminal show 36, or something stranger? Commit to a guess before you run.
121212. Here's the trap: input() always hands you text, even when the text looks like a number. And text times 3 means repeat the letters three times. The wand did exactly what you said. It always does.
You should see 
How many sparks for practice? 12
121212
Chunk 8 · int() at the gateE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Convert at the gate, then write the scroll

Escape the trap by converting text to a number the moment it arrives. The Academy calls this "convert at the gate." Then an f-string writes one tidy scroll line: put an f before the quotes, and anything inside { } gets swapped for its value.

name = input("State your name, Apprentice: ")
tokens = int(input("How many spell tokens? "))
sparks = tokens * 12
print(f"Scroll of Registration: {name} holds {tokens} tokens = {sparks} sparks.")
Predict If the apprentice is Sam with 3 tokens, what exact line prints? Trace it first: what does sparks hold before line 4 runs?
sparks holds 36 (3 × 12), so it prints Scroll of Registration: Sam holds 3 tokens = 36 sparks. The int() wrapper turns the typed "3" into the number 3 before it ever lands in the vessel, so the math works. Now finish your Grimoire trace for this program: four lines, four rows.
You should see 
State your name, Apprentice: Sam
How many spell tokens? 3
Scroll of Registration: Sam holds 3 tokens = 36 sparks.
Hinge question · stop here until you're sureE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

The twice-filled vessel

A program runs these two lines, in order: charge = 40 and then charge = 25.
What does charge hold now: 40, 25, both, or 65?

25, and only 25. A vessel holds one value, and = pours over whatever was there. If you said "both" or "40," re-read Chunk 5 before moving on. If you said 25, you're cleared for the build.
Build brief · The Registration Scroll

Your scroll, fully inked

Build a Registration Scroll program that:

1. Prints the Academy seal (2–3 lines).
2. Asks the apprentice's name with input().
3. Asks how many spell tokens they carry, converting with int().
4. Computes their sparks (each token holds 12) and prints one scroll line with an f-string.

Useful pieces: print() · input() · int() · * · f"..." · # comments to label each section.

Suggested start: get the seal printing first. Then add one question at a time. House law: one change per run.

# --- Academy seal ---
print("* * * * * * * * * * *")
print(" ACADEMY  REGISTRATION")
print("* * * * * * * * * * *")

# --- Registration desk ---
name = input("State your name, Apprentice: ")
tokens = int(input("How many spell tokens do you carry? "))
sparks = tokens * 12
print(f"Welcome to the Academy, {name}!")
print(f"Scroll of Registration: {tokens} tokens = {sparks} sparks")
Yours doesn't have to match this. It has to run, and you have to be able to say what each line does.

Choose your exit gate

Floor · approaching the standard

Your Academy seal prints correctly, and you can misfire print on purpose and fix it by reading the traceback last line first.

Middle · meets the standard

Your full Registration Scroll runs (name, tokens, int() conversion, f-string scroll line) and your Grimoire trace for the four-line program is complete and correct.

Stretch · exceeds the standard

Fetch the sealing powder: in the VS Code terminal run pip install pyfiglet, then crown your scroll with a giant banner: import pyfiglet and print(pyfiglet.figlet_format("ACADEMY")). Then add a second question (how many practice hours?) and fold both numbers into one grand scroll line.
