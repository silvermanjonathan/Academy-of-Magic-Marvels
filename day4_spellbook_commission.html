Chunk 1 · Write it once, cast it twice

def: a spell stored, not spoken

def writes a spell into your spellbook. Writing it down casts nothing. The spell fires only when you call its name.

def academy_greeting():
    print("* * * * * * * * *")
    print("  SPELL PREPARED")
    print("* * * * * * * * *")

academy_greeting()
academy_greeting()
Predict How many banners print? And if you deleted the two calls at the bottom and ran only the def block, what would print?
Two banners, one per call. Delete the calls and nothing prints at all. A def is ink in a book: nothing happens until you call it. Most apprentices guess wrong here, which is exactly why we run the experiment.

Chunk 2 · The blank in the spell

A parameter, filled at casting time

A parameter is a vessel that belongs to the spell: empty in the book, filled the moment you cast.

def cast(spell):
    print(f"Casting {spell}!")

cast("emberlight")
cast("frostveil")
Predict Write both output lines exactly. During the second call, what value sits inside the vessel spell?
During the second call, spell holds "frostveil". Same spell, refilled vessel. Write the def once and you can cast it as many times as you like, which is the reason functions exist.

Casting emberlight!
Casting frostveil!
Chunk 3 · Two blanks, filled in order

Position pours the vessels

With two parameters, the wand fills them by position: first argument into the first vessel, second into the second. Watch what the swapped call does.

def brew(potion, doses):
    print(f"Prepare {doses} doses of {potion}.")

brew("moonpetal tonic", 3)
brew(3, "moonpetal tonic")
Predict Write both output lines exactly. Is the second call a misfire, or something quieter and worse?
No misfire. The wand happily pours 3 into potion and the tonic into doses, then prints nonsense with a straight face. Argument order carries the meaning. And a program that's quietly wrong is more dangerous than one that crashes; remember this one tomorrow at the Clinic.
You should see 
Prepare 3 doses of moonpetal tonic.
Prepare moonpetal tonic doses of 3.

Chunk 5 · First Casting of the engine5.G.A.2

Wandlight: run it before you touch it

pygame has been on your wand since Day 2, and its five-beat loop since Day 3. Open wandlight_pygame.py, or copy every line below into a file with that exact name. Change nothing. Run it. That's the gate: the engine has to run unmodified on your wand before any customizing begins.

import pygame, random

pygame.init()
WIDTH = 640
HEIGHT = 480
screen = pygame.display.set_mode((WIDTH, HEIGHT))
pygame.display.set_caption("Wandlight")
clock = pygame.time.Clock()

# ---------- THE ENCHANTMENT PANEL (your customizations live here) ----------
SPARK_COLORS = [(240, 180, 41), (250, 245, 235), (196, 120, 255)]
SPARK_COUNT = 40
RISE_SPEED = 2
FRAMES = 600            # 600 frames at 60 per second = a 10-second casting

WAND_TIP_X = WIDTH // 2
WAND_TIP_Y = 400
SKY = (45, 27, 78)
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Parallel lists: spark number i is xs[i], ys[i], speeds[i], sizes[i], colors[i]
xs = []
ys = []
speeds = []
sizes = []
colors = []

for i in range(SPARK_COUNT):
    xs.append(WAND_TIP_X + random.randint(-6, 6))
    ys.append(WAND_TIP_Y + random.randint(0, 30))
    speeds.append(RISE_SPEED + random.randint(0, 2))
    sizes.append(random.randint(2, 5))
    colors.append(random.choice(SPARK_COLORS))

def draw_wand(surface):
    pygame.draw.line(surface, (240, 180, 41),
                     (WAND_TIP_X + 60, HEIGHT - 20),
                     (WAND_TIP_X, WAND_TIP_Y), 6)
    pygame.draw.circle(surface, (250, 245, 235),
                       (WAND_TIP_X, WAND_TIP_Y), 5)

for frame in range(FRAMES):
    pygame.event.pump()          # let the window breathe
    screen.fill(SKY)
    draw_wand(screen)

    for i in range(SPARK_COUNT):
        ys[i] = ys[i] - speeds[i]              # rising: y gets SMALLER
        xs[i] = xs[i] + random.randint(-1, 1)  # the wobble
        if ys[i] < 0:                          # past the top: respawn at the tip
            xs[i] = WAND_TIP_X + random.randint(-6, 6)
            ys[i] = WAND_TIP_Y
        pygame.draw.circle(screen, colors[i], (xs[i], ys[i]), sizes[i])

    pygame.display.flip()
    clock.tick(60)

pygame.quit()
Predict Three predictions before you run. (1) How many seconds will the window stay open? Compute it from the Enchantment Panel. (2) The wand tip sits at (320, 400). As a spark rises, does its y value get bigger or smaller? (3) What happens to a spark whose y passes 0?
(1) Ten seconds: 600 frames ÷ 60 per second. The casting is countable, like every loop this week. (2) Smaller. On a screen, y counts downward from the top edge, so rising means y shrinking toward 0. That's why the update says ys[i] - speeds[i]. (3) It respawns at the wand tip and rises again. Reading what a coordinate pair means in this particular situation is the skill the hinge is about to check.

Hinge question · stop here until you're sure5.G.A.2

The rising number

One frame runs the line ys[i] = ys[i] - speeds[i].
After it, is the spark drawn higher or lower on the screen, and is the number in ys[i] bigger or smaller than before?

Higher on the screen; smaller in the vessel. If you said "higher and bigger," you're using math-class axes on a screen that counts from the top. Re-read Chunk 5's second reveal before accepting the Commission. If you got both, the Academy is ready to commission you.

Your Marvel: a customized Wandlight

Deadline law Your Marvel runs by the end of Day 4. Tomorrow's Counter-Curse Clinic lifts curses; it doesn't host first castings. Worst case at day's end, revert to the clean engine plus your one safest change. Nobody walks into Day 5 with nothing running.

Step 0: the Spell Schematic (before any customizing). On your schematic sheet, draw the engine as boxes and arrows: setup at the top, the frame loop as a ring, and inside the ring the three beats: update the sparks, the respawn decision, draw. Label where SPARK_COUNT, RISE_SPEED, and ys[i] live. Your teacher signs it. Then customize.

The four requirements. Each is a tool from this week, and house law holds: one change per run, predict before each.

1. Your colors. Rebuild SPARK_COLORS as your own list of 3–5 color tuples.
2. Your numbers. Change SPARK_COUNT and RISE_SPEED; predict each effect before the run.
3. Your turn of night. Replace screen.fill(SKY) in the frame loop with a flat gate that darkens the sky after frame 300.
4. Your own spell. Write one new def that draws something of yours, and call it in the loop.

# Requirement 3 — replace screen.fill(SKY) inside the loop with:
    if frame > 300:
        screen.fill((16, 8, 34))     # midnight falls halfway through
    else:
        screen.fill(SKY)

# Requirement 4 — a new spell, defined near draw_wand:
def draw_sigil(surface):
    pygame.draw.circle(surface, (240, 180, 41),
                       (WAND_TIP_X, WAND_TIP_Y), 40, 2)

# ...and called inside the loop, right after draw_wand(screen):
    draw_sigil(surface=screen)
A flat gate, a named spell, a call. Nothing here is new syntax, only new purpose. Make the sigil yours: a ring, a crescent of circles, whatever you can draw with circles and lines. Update your schematic to show where it fires.

Deliberately not covered here: return values: every spell in the book acts by drawing or printing, and apprentices who reach for return get one-on-one support, by program-wide design. The casting ships as a countable for frame in range(600) so its length stays a prediction; while was taught on Day 3, and converting the engine to the while running watch is the sanctioned stretch, not the default. Sprites, image files, and classes are excluded program-wide. Event handling is reduced to pygame.event.pump() on purpose: the window closes itself when the counted casting completes.
