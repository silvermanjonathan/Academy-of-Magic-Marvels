Chunk 1 · Pattern: the accumulatorE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

The gathering charm

The most useful pattern in programming: start a vessel at 0, then let a loop pour into it, one pass at a time.

sparks = [12, 7, 20]
total = 0
for s in sparks:
    total = total + s
print("Sparks gathered:", total)
Predict Trace it on your Grimoire sheet: what does total hold after each pass? What prints at the end?
total goes 0, then 12, then 19, then 39. The line total = total + s reads strangely at first: pour into the vessel its own contents plus one more spark. Every running total you have ever seen, from game scores to shopping carts, is this exact charm.

Sparks gathered: 39
Chunk 2 · The mirror questionE5-PRO-RD-?? ⚠

== asks; = pours

One equals sign pours a value in. Two equals signs ask a question: are these the same? The wand answers True or False.

word = "ember"
print(word == "ember")
print(word == "Ember")
Predict Two answers will print. What are they, and why do they differ?
True, then False. The wand matches letters exactly, and a capital E is a different letter. Mixing up = and == is one of the most common misfires in all of programming; now you can name both halves.

False
Chunk 3 · Pattern: the counter with a gateE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

Count only the strong ones

An accumulator that adds 1 instead of the value, and only when a gate says yes.

sparks = [12, 7, 20, 9]
strong = 0
for s in sparks:
    if s > 10:
        strong = strong + 1
print("Strong sparks:", strong)
Predict Which sparks pass the gate? What does strong hold at the end?
12 and 20 pass; 7 and 9 don't. strong ends at 2. Notice the shape: a loop, a gate inside it, a counter inside the gate. Three Day-1 tools, one new pattern.

Strong sparks: 2
Chunk 4 · Pattern: best-so-farE5-PRO-VD-?? ⚠

The brightest spark

Keep a champion vessel. Every pass, a challenger arrives; the gate decides whether the champion is replaced.

sparks = [12, 7, 20, 9]
brightest = 0
for s in sparks:
    if s > brightest:
        brightest = s
print("Brightest spark:", brightest)
Predict Trace brightest through all four passes. On which passes does it change, and what does it hold at the end?
It changes on pass 1 (0 becomes 12) and pass 3 (12 becomes 20); passes 2 and 4 leave it alone because 7 and 9 lose the challenge. Final answer: 20. Swap the > for < and the same pattern finds the dimmest spark instead.

Brightest spark: 20
Chunk 5 · First LightE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Open a window in nine lines

You installed pygame at the door. Now the moment: a real window, a violet sky, one golden orb. Save this as its own casting and run it.

import pygame

pygame.init()
screen = pygame.display.set_mode((640, 480))
pygame.display.set_caption("First Light")
screen.fill((45, 27, 78))
pygame.draw.circle(screen, (240, 180, 41), (320, 240), 60)
pygame.display.flip()
pygame.time.wait(3000)
pygame.quit()
Predict How long will the window stay open, and what makes it close? The orb sits at (320, 240) in a 640-by-480 window: where on the screen is that?
Three seconds: wait(3000) counts in milliseconds, then the program reaches pygame.quit() and the window closes itself. And (320, 240) is dead center, half of 640 and half of 480. The three color numbers are red, green, and blue, 0 to 255; the sky is the Academy's own violet. Nine lines, and you are officially a graphics programmer.
You should see 
A violet window titled "First Light" with one golden orb
in the center. It closes by itself after 3 seconds.
Chunk 6 · A rule places the orbs5.OA.B.3

The constellation rule

Yesterday a rule wove runes in the terminal. The same idea places orbs in a window: the rule 70 + i * 100 turns the loop number into a position.

import pygame

pygame.init()
screen = pygame.display.set_mode((640, 480))
pygame.display.set_caption("The Constellation Rule")
screen.fill((45, 27, 78))
for i in range(6):
    pygame.draw.circle(screen, (240, 180, 41), (70 + i * 100, 240), 30)
pygame.display.flip()
pygame.time.wait(3000)
pygame.quit()
Predict Fill the ordered-pairs table on your Grimoire sheet: (i, x position) for every pass. Where does the first orb sit, and how far apart are neighbors?
The pairs are (0, 70), (1, 170), (2, 270), (3, 370), (4, 470), (5, 570): start at 70, step by 100. Two patterns move together, the loop number and the position, and the rule is the bridge between them. Change the 100 and the whole constellation breathes in or out.
You should see 
Six golden orbs in a level row across the middle
of the window, evenly spaced.
Chunk 7 · A loom inside a loom5.OA.B.3

The star wall

Put one loop inside another and the rule gains a second direction: rows and columns.

import pygame

pygame.init()
screen = pygame.display.set_mode((640, 480))
pygame.display.set_caption("The Star Wall")
screen.fill((45, 27, 78))
for row in range(3):
    for col in range(5):
        pygame.draw.circle(screen, (250, 245, 235), (70 + col * 125, 90 + row * 150), 12)
pygame.display.flip()
pygame.time.wait(3000)
pygame.quit()
Predict How many stars in total? For the star at row 2, col 0: what are its x and y?
Fifteen stars: the inner loop runs all five of its columns for each of the three rows, 3 × 5. The star at row 2, col 0 sits at x = 70 and y = 90 + 2 × 150 = 390, low on the screen because y grows downward. Loops inside loops are how every grid you've ever seen gets drawn.
You should see 
A 3-by-5 wall of small parchment-white stars,
evenly spaced across the violet sky.
Chunk 8 · A list feeds the windowE5-PRO-PD-?? ⚠

Orbs sized by the shelf

The bridge to Day 4: a list holds the data, a loop reads it, and the window shows it. Spark i gets size sizes[i].

import pygame

pygame.init()
screen = pygame.display.set_mode((640, 480))
pygame.display.set_caption("The Ledger, Drawn")
sizes = [10, 25, 40, 25, 10]
screen.fill((45, 27, 78))
for i in range(5):
    pygame.draw.circle(screen, (240, 180, 41), (70 + i * 125, 240), sizes[i])
pygame.display.flip()
pygame.time.wait(3000)
pygame.quit()
Predict Which orb is biggest, and where does it sit? What one change makes the row shrink toward the middle instead?
The middle orb, at x = 320, because sizes[2] is 40. To flip the shape, reorder the list: [40, 25, 10, 25, 40]. One list drives the whole picture, which is exactly how the Day 4 engine works: its sparks live in lists, and the loop reads them out. You've now seen the engine's whole trick.
You should see 
Five golden orbs in a row that swell toward
the large center orb, then shrink again.

The gathering trace

Without running it: sparks = [5, 10, 2], then the accumulator pattern pours each into total.
What does total hold after each pass, and what prints at the end?

5, then 15, then 17. If you said 2 (only the last value) you traced a pour; the accumulator adds. If you said 17, the Codex is yours; the same trace works for every pattern today, because they are all the accumulator wearing different hats.
Build brief · The Ledger & the Constellation

Count it, then draw it

Part A, the Ledger of Sparks (terminal): make your own list of 5–6 spark values, then report in f-strings: the total (accumulator), how many are over 15 (counter with a gate), and the brightest (best-so-far).

Part B, the Constellation (window): draw your list as a row of orbs, one per value, using your spark values as the sizes. Keep values between 5 and 45 so the orbs fit.

Suggested start: Part A first, one pattern per run. House law: one change per run.

import pygame

sparks = [18, 9, 33, 21, 12]

# --- Part A: the ledger ---
total = 0
strong = 0
brightest = 0
for s in sparks:
    total = total + s
    if s > 15:
        strong = strong + 1
    if s > brightest:
        brightest = s
print(f"Total sparks: {total}")
print(f"Over 15: {strong}")
print(f"Brightest: {brightest}")

# --- Part B: the constellation ---
pygame.init()
screen = pygame.display.set_mode((640, 480))
pygame.display.set_caption("My Constellation")
screen.fill((45, 27, 78))
for i in range(5):
    pygame.draw.circle(screen, (240, 180, 41), (70 + i * 125, 240), sparks[i])
pygame.display.flip()
pygame.time.wait(4000)
pygame.quit()
Three patterns share one loop here; yours may use three separate loops, which is just as correct and easier to trace.
